The property is not silent

The garden is already telling you what it can support.

Light moves across it. Heat collects against some surfaces and leaves others protected. Existing plants reveal patterns of exposure, shelter and care. Soil holds moisture differently from one part of the property to another. The first task is not to impose a style—it is to learn how to read what is already there.

What the reading does

Observation creates a trustworthy starting point.

The 60-minute visit is a structured reading of the property. It establishes what should be considered before planting and whether a simple self-directed response, further specialist investigation or a complete planting design is the responsible next movement.

Sun path & shade

Where light moves through the property and how walls, buildings, canopy and seasonal change alter planting opportunity.

Exposure & microclimate

Visible heat, wind, shelter and hard-surface conditions that make one part of the garden behave differently from another.

Existing vegetation

A profile of established planting and the clues it provides about present conditions, structure and future opportunity.

Simple hydration observations

Point-in-time observations across the property—not laboratory soil testing, drainage diagnosis or irrigation assessment.

How the property is used

The reading also considers the practical reality of the household: movement, outdoor use, broad maintenance awareness and the level of care the future planting must be able to live within.

Where planting has leverage

The visit identifies broad areas where canopy, habitat, flowers, edible planting, screening or outdoor living may be supported—and where preparation or another kind of advice may be required first.

The lasting result

A property record you can keep using.

Within 14 calendar days, you receive a full-colour bound physical report and a digital copy of your Garden Conditions & Planting Opportunity Report.

The report is not designed to make further work compulsory. It gives you a truthful baseline for future garden decisions—even when you choose to urban-green the property gradually and independently.

Observed site conditions

A structured record of the conditions visible during the visit.

Seasonal orientation

An interpretation of how sun and exposure are expected to shift through the year.

Existing vegetation profile

Notes on the planting already present and the role it may play in later decisions.

Annotated opportunity plan

A readable property diagram identifying broad planting opportunities and constraints.

Planting-function zones

Areas that may suit canopy, habitat, flowers, edible planting, screening or outdoor use.

Future decision baseline

A reference to return to when purchasing plants or considering later property work.

A good fit

The reading is appropriate when you want to understand before acting.

  • Your residential garden feels bare, hot, exposed, underplanted or difficult to interpret.
  • You want a property record for informed self-directed planting.
  • You may want a complete planting design, but want the site evidence established first.
  • An authorised property decision-maker can attend and the relevant outdoor areas can be accessed safely.
Outside this service

Some questions require a different professional.

A Garden Site Reading is not laboratory soil testing, arboricultural assessment, engineering advice, drainage or irrigation diagnosis, contamination assessment, a hardscape consultation or a planting-design workshop.

Properties in active construction or demolition, with unresolved access or safety conditions, or seeking structural and specialist advice may be deferred or referred before a visit is offered.

Frequently asked questions.

Is this a planting-design consultation?
No. The visit reads and records the property. It may establish the foundation for planting design, but the design itself is a separate later service with its own proposal and agreement.
Will you tell me exactly which plants to buy?
The report identifies broad planting opportunities and functions. A resolved species schedule, quantities and placement plan belong to the separate planting-design service.
Is the report useful if I do not continue with the studio?
Yes. Its purpose is to give you a durable property record and annotated opportunity plan you can use for gradual, more informed garden decisions.
When is payment requested?
Only after Mopani Studio has reviewed the request, confirmed the property is suitable and offered appointment options. Submitting the form does not create a booking or require payment.
Can a tenant request a reading?
A tenant or occupant may submit a request, but written authority to make decisions about the garden is required before an accepted visit is confirmed.
